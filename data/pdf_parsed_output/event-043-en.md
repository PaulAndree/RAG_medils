Second VERDANT privacy session to compare the risk and utility of 15-minute and hourly aggregated data and establish provisional conditions for exporting real data during the pilot.

Andrés Beltrán: Inés and Sergio prepared the requested comparison. Inés, what additional utility do 15-minute data provide?
Inés Ferrer: They improve peak detection and flexibility estimation. Hourly data are sufficient for daily balancing and community comparison.
Sergio Vidal: In testing, hourly aggregation preserves more than 96 percent of the information required for the initial indicators.
Héctor Molina: The risk changes significantly. In small facilities, a 15-minute series can reveal opening hours, absences and equipment use.
Carmen Rey: SolCentro can aggregate locally before export. We can also remove meter identifiers and use a temporary facility code.
Bruno Silva: For the international test, we only need hourly values and quality metadata.
Andrés Beltrán: I propose authorizing hourly export under four conditions: local aggregation, a temporary pseudonym, an approved purpose and deletion after ninety days.
Héctor Molina: I would add a minimum of three facilities per exported dataset and a prohibition on redistribution.
Inés Ferrer: For experiments requiring 15-minute data, could we run the algorithm inside SolCentro and export only aggregated results?
Carmen Rey: Technically, yes, provided that we review the code and its output.
Andrés Beltrán: That becomes the preferred route. Export of raw 15-minute series is not authorized. Hourly data may be exported under the six agreed conditions, and local processing of 15-minute data may produce aggregated output subject to review.
