First LUMEN review of uncertain dates to agree how to represent approximate years, intervals, inferred dates and missing information without converting archival estimates into exact dates.

Lucía Navarro: We have examples such as 'circa 1910', 'between 1936 and 1939', 'before 1952' and dates inferred from the paper type. We cannot convert all of them into a single date.
Eva Calderón: The system must retain both the original expression and the normalized interpretation. An approximate date must never be displayed to users as exact.
Mateo Ruiz: I propose storing a minimum and maximum interval, an uncertainty type and the record's textual note.
Mireia Soler: That model works for ranges and approximations. For 'before' or 'after', one of the limits would remain open.
Yara Ben Salem: We also need consistent bilingual labels: approximate, inferred, uncertain, before and after. The translation must reflect the archival meaning, not only the linguistic form.
Sergio Vidal: For indexing, we can use the normalized interval, but we must retain the original expression as evidence and display it in the interface.
Eva Calderón: For inferred dates, the record must also state who made the inference and which source supports it.
Lucía Navarro: We therefore agree on five elements: original expression, normalized interval, uncertainty type, provenance of the estimate and the person responsible for the inference when applicable.
Mateo Ruiz: I will prepare test examples for each pattern.
Mireia Soler: I will draft the proposed classes and properties for the next review.
