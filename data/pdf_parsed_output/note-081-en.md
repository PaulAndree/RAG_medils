Faithfulness measures whether every claim in an answer is supported by the cited sources. Completeness measures whether the answer includes all elements needed to resolve the question. An answer may be faithful but incomplete, or complete but unfaithful.

Faithful but incomplete example: in response to a question about the requirements and deadline for reporting a trip, the answer correctly states the ten-working-day deadline but omits the mandatory supporting documents. Complete but unfaithful example: it lists the deadline, invoice, and boarding pass but adds a prior approval requirement that is absent from the rules. Faithful and complete example: it contains every requested requirement, and each is supported by valid evidence.

The rubric will use atomic claims. Faithfulness will be calculated as the proportion of supported claims, and completeness as the proportion of expected elements that are present. Irrelevant claims will also reduce the quality score even when supported. Tomás will prepare twenty adjudicated examples for evaluator training.
