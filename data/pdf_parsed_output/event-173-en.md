First post-closure review of the ORION data dictionary to document canonical fields, legacy aliases, local extensions and provenance rules before final project archiving.

Elena Martín: ORION ended in 2025, but the preservation file needs a clear and reusable dictionary. We are not reopening project development.
Mireia Soler: The dictionary contains 126 fields across person, organization, project, output, funding and affiliation entities.
Sergio Vidal: Ninety-four fields are canonical, twenty-one are legacy aliases from institutional sources and eleven are local extensions that must not be treated as a general standard.
Diego Ramos: For person profiles, the main problems are duplicate identifiers, alternate names and affiliations with incomplete periods.
Nuria Castaño: Preservation requires type, cardinality, vocabulary, source, extraction date and transformation rule for every field.
Clara Ibáñez: We must also indicate which fields contain restricted information and their retention period.
Mireia Soler: I propose marking aliases as deprecatedAlias and linking them to the canonical field without deleting them from historical records.
Sergio Vidal: Local extensions will use their own namespace and include a use justification. This prevents future reuse from interpreting them as part of the common model.
Elena Martín: Do any field definitions remain ambiguous?
Diego Ramos: Seven: two affiliation fields, three funding fields and two output-contribution fields. We will prepare positive and negative examples.
Elena Martín: We approve the 94/21/11 classification, preservation of aliases and the extension namespace. The seven ambiguous fields will be resolved in the second review.
