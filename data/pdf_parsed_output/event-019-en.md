First LUMEN terminology workshop to compare archival uses of creator, author and producer, identify unsuitable equivalences in the preliminary guide and agree which cases require distinct Spanish and English definitions.

Lucía Navarro: The preliminary guide uses creator as a general term and presents author and producer as alternatives depending on the object type.
Eva Calderón: That simplification is problematic for the archive. One person may be the intellectual author of a letter, while another organization is responsible for producing the digital copy or assembling the file.
Mateo Ruiz: In several records, the producer field refers to the institution that generated and maintained the fonds, not to the person who created each individual document.
Diego Ramos: If we treat all three terms as equivalent, entity linking will merge different roles and queries will return incorrect results.
Mireia Soler: We can model a general contribution relation and specialize it into authorship, material creation and archival production.
Yara Ben Salem: In English, we must also avoid automatically translating productor as producer. Archival producer or records creator may be appropriate, but the choice depends on the definition.
Eva Calderón: I prefer records creator for the entity responsible for creating or accumulating the fonds in the course of its functions. Producer should be reserved for technical or audiovisual cases when the record specifies it.
Lucía Navarro: We will therefore not close a single equivalence today. Mateo will collect twenty archive examples, Diego will analyze their effect on entity linking and Yara will propose bilingual definitions.
Eva Calderón: Agreed. The current guide should remain marked as a draft until the distinction is resolved.
